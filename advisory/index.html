02 The process

Six phases, each closed by a decision you sign off.

A direct platform introduction typically completes in three to six months; a full competitive process takes six to nine months. Each phase ends at a clear gate, and nothing moves to the next stage without your written approval.

Two rules hold throughout. No buyer learns your firm's name before signing a non-disclosure agreement (or clearing a written conflict check with your consent). No buyer is granted exclusivity before a written letter of intent settles price, structure, earn-out metrics, the working capital approach and your role after completion.

01

Written qualification and NDA

Days 1 to 5

Everything starts asynchronously in writing by email. We confirm four headline metrics (revenue, adjusted EBITDA, recurring mix, and concentration) under a one-page mutual NDA, with zero calendar friction.

  • 1-page Mutual Confidentiality Agreement
  • Four-point written qualification check
  • Selection of path: zero-fee buyer fast-track or competitive sell-side process
  • Normalized EBITDA and add-back review

GateSigned NDA and written confirmation of mandate path.

02

Materials and target clearance

Weeks 1 to 3

For a direct platform introduction, we run a 3-business-day blind conflict check with the acquirer before disclosing your company name. For a competitive sale, we build the anonymous teaser and financial summary.

  • Anonymous two-page investment teaser
  • Normalized financial summary and recurring revenue schedule
  • 3-day buyer conflict-clearance protocol (on fast-track intros)
  • Virtual data room index

GateWritten owner approval of materials or target clearance confirmation.

03

Introductions and marketing

Weeks 3 to 8

On the fast-track path, we make a direct double opt-in written introduction with a dedicated first-look window. On a competitive mandate, approved acquirers are approached in a coordinated wave.

  • Double opt-in email introduction or coordinated outreach wave
  • Mutual NDAs executed before identity disclosure
  • Financial summary and memorandum released
  • Asynchronous written management Q&A

GateQualified buyer engagement and Q&A completion.

04

Indications of interest and LOI

Weeks 6 to 12

Buyers submit written indications of interest (IOIs) followed by focused leadership discussions and binding letters of intent (LOIs) detailing price, cash at close, working capital, and transition terms.

  • Written IOIs evaluated on headline price and net cash at close
  • Focused executive alignment sessions
  • LOIs negotiated on cash at completion, escrow, earn-out and rollover equity

Exclusivity and confirmatory diligence

Weeks 10 to 18

The selected acquirer conducts financial, tax, legal, and technical diligence against a pre-organized virtual data room, keeping the price anchored to what the LOI set.

  • Structured Virtual Data Room (VDR) management
  • Quality of earnings (QoE) and technical diligence coordination
  • Re-trade defense grounded in pre-disclosed records
  • Disclosure schedules prepared with your counsel

Signing and completion

At the close

Legal counsel finalizes the definitive purchase agreement, third-party consents are completed, and closing funds are wired directly through escrow.

  • Definitive Share or Asset Purchase Agreement
  • Partner programme and key contract consents
  • Closing funds flow and escrow distribution
  • Structured founder and team transition

03 Value drivers

What drives value in an enterprise technology firm.

Acquirers pay for earnings they can rely on after completion. These are the six areas they test hardest, and where preparation does the most for the price.

01

Recurring revenue

Managed services, support renewals and subscriptions earn a higher multiple than project work and licence resale. Separating and evidencing them is often the largest single lever on price.

02

Retention and concentration

Gross and net revenue retention, keeping the largest single client under 25% of revenue, and contract terms that survive a change of control.

03

Vendor and partner position

Partner tier, certifications and programme status, and whether they transfer to a new owner. Dependence on a single vendor is priced as risk.

04

People

Certified consultants and engineers are much of what an acquirer buys. Dependence on the owner is the most common discount, and one of the most fixable.

05

Technology and IP

Own products, add-ons and integrations can be an asset or a liability. Code quality, technical debt and security posture are reviewed before the buyer's team does it.

06

Financial hygiene

Monthly management accounts, clean revenue recognition and an understood working capital cycle shorten diligence and protect the price.

04 Sale readiness

Not ready yet? Prepare for the sale you want.

The strongest prices go to firms prepared well before they go to market. A readiness engagement, typically 12 to 24 months ahead, works through the value drivers and leaves you with a business that diligence confirms rather than discounts.

  • A written value ladder. Evidenced, achievable and stretch values, and what moves each.
  • Vendor technology diligence. The technology estate reviewed as an acquirer's team would review it.
  • A fix list ranked by effect on price. What to change, what to document and what to disclose.
  • A clear point to go to market. Set by the evidence, not by the calendar.

06 Terms

Fees, confidentiality and single-side alignment.

Fees (Zero Retainers)

100% success-fee based, with no upfront retainer and no monthly fees. On a buyer-sponsored fast-track introduction, the acquiring platform pays our entire 3.0% fee at closing, so you pay $0. On an exclusive sell-side mandate, our success fee is paid from proceeds at completion.

Confidentiality

Every conversation starts under a 1-page Mutual NDA. Your firm's name is never shared with any acquirer without your explicit written permission, and staff, clients and vendors learn of a transaction only when you decide.

Single-side fee rule

We never collect a dual fee from both buyer and seller on the same transaction. Which side pays the completion fee is stated upfront in writing before any introduction occurs.

Your other advisers

Your own lawyer drafts and negotiates the definitive purchase agreement and your accountant advises on tax structure. I coordinate them, and the buyer's diligence team, to one timetable.

07 Questions

Questions owners ask first.

How does the zero-fee buyer introduction work?

We hold signed buy-side finder agreements with institutional acquisition platforms (such as Alpine-backed MSP and ERP holding companies). When your practice matches their buy-box ($3M+ revenue, $500k+ EBITDA, 12+ staff, >50% recurring revenue) and you do not have an existing broker, the buyer pays our 3.0% introduction fee at closing. You pay $0, sign no exclusive listing contract, and confirm fit asynchronously in writing by email.

What is my firm worth?

Less a rule-of-thumb multiple than what a specific platform or strategic buyer can do with it. A firm with contracted recurring revenue, low customer concentration (<25% in the largest client) and a team that operates independently of the founder commands a strong multiple of normalized EBITDA. A short email exchange gives a first written view tested against active buyer mandates.

Do I have to get on introductory phone calls?

No. Initial qualification, NDA execution, normalized numbers review, and buyer clearance are handled 100% in writing by email so we never waste your calendar on speculative calls.

Do I have to sell 100% of the business?

No. Permanent-hold platforms and private equity sponsors offer both 100% cash buyouts and majority recapitalizations where you retain rollover equity or stay on in a paid leadership transition.

Will my staff and clients find out?

Not from our process. Nothing identifying is ever shared without your explicit written permission and a signed NDA. When and how staff, clients and vendors are told is your decision, typically at completion.

Why an adviser with an enterprise platforms background?

Because much of the value, and most of the risk, in an IT, ERP or software firm is technical: vendor dependency, partner tier transferability, proprietary IP, technical debt and security posture. Understanding them lets me position your strengths and defend the price in diligence.

Are you regulated?

I am an independent M&A adviser, not a registered broker-dealer and not a firm authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority. Mandates are limited to the sale of a controlling interest in a private company to an active operating buyer. Full details are on the disclosures page.
