05 The difference

An M&A practice that reads the technology the way an acquirer does.

In an enterprise technology firm much of the value sits where a generalist broker cannot see it: how durable the managed services contracts are, how far the firm depends on one vendor's partner programme, whether its own IP is an asset or a liability, and how much revenue survives a change of control.

Having designed, built and operated enterprise technology since 1998, I identify what a buyer's diligence team will test before the buyer does, and present the business in the exact financial and operational terms investment committees use to price it.

  • Recurring revenue quality. Managed services, support renewals and publisher residuals separated from project work and one-off licence resale.
  • Vendor and partner position. Tier, certifications, and whether publisher margins transfer cleanly on a change of control.
  • Technology and IP. Proprietary vertical add-ons, connectors, code quality and technical debt audited upfront.
  • Customer and contract risk. Concentration ceilings, renewal terms and change-of-control clauses reviewed prior to market.
